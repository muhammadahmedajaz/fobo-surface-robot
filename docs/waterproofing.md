# XPS, hot-glue and silicone packaging

The team needed to protect ordinary motors and servos within a small budget. Ahmed described the method below from the actual build. It used foam around the actuator body and hot glue/silicone to close the surrounding spaces.

![Conceptual actuator packaging](../assets/figures/actuator-packaging.png)

The drawing explains the materials and arrangement. It does not specify a shaft-seal geometry or manufacturing dimensions.

## DC motor packaging

1. Make a motor-sized space in an XPS foam piece.
2. Place the motor inside, with its shaft extending outside the foam.
3. Fill and seal around the motor using hot glue and silicone, leaving the shaft available to drive the propeller.

The foam surrounds the motor body. The description does not establish a separate lip seal, O-ring or gland assembly. The report's epoxy-around-shaft wording is not part of the builder-confirmed procedure.

## MG996R servo packaging

1. Make a servo-sized cavity in XPS foam.
2. Put the servo into that cavity.
3. Apply glue and silicone around the servo to seal it into the foam packaging.

The servo's rotating output still couples to the motor mount. Detailed photographs and dimensions of that interface are not available in the recovered construction material.

## Hull and electronics bay

The body was first shaped in XPS and then covered with fiberglass. Earlier construction records describe a central electronics bay, a separate waterproof box and silicone sealing around enclosure interfaces. The battery was inside the central compartment.

Glue, silicone and fiberglass-resin product names, quantities and curing conditions were not recorded. They should not be inferred from the generic material names.

## What happened in the final round

**One servo failed when water entered it during the final round.** Ahmed reports that the arrangement otherwise worked well through the project. No diagnosis identifies whether entry occurred at the case, cable or rotating-output interface.

This failure is part of the build result. The packaging offered useful protection during operation, while the final-round failure shows that its sealing was incomplete. A replication should improve and verify the actuator protection rather than treating the prototype as a rated underwater enclosure.

No ingress-protection rating, guaranteed submersion duration or depth rating is claimed. The [gallery](gallery.md) documents the real prototype and its operation.
